\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:Introduction}
This chapter presents and justifies the methodological approach adopted to examine the influence of organisational culture and leadership on the return on investment (ROI) realised from AI coding assistants in software engineering teams. Given the complexity of the constructs under investigation, organisational culture, leadership, the assimilation quality of AI coding assistants, and ROI, it was essential that the research design be methodologically sound, so as to support the validity and generalisability of the findings. \textcite{Creswell2018} argues that the research design adopted should align closely with the research question(s) and the underlying theoretical framework. As this study sought to test relationships between constructs, to assess a mediating effect, and to do so while accounting for the measurement structure of each construct, a quantitative design combining preliminary correlational analysis with partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS-SEM) was adopted. The chapter proceeds by outlining the research philosophy, research design, research hypotheses, population and sampling strategy, measurement and operationalisation of constructs, data collection procedure, data analysis techniques, research limitations, and the ethical considerations that guided the research.

\section{Research Philosophy}
\label{sec:Research Philosophy}
This paper is based on a post-positivist epistemology, which posits that social phenomena can be studied in a systematic manner, although measurement can never be perfect \autocite{Creswell2018}.

\subsection{Suitability of Post-Positivism for This Study}
\label{sec:Post-positivist theory suits this research because it will}
The hypothesis testing process measures to what extent the relationships described in the conceptual framework are valid based on data. In quantitative analysis, hypotheses originate from theoretical frameworks presented in literature and are compared with empirical information. Consequently, researchers can determine whether or not links exist between different factors including organisational culture, leadership, the introduction of artificial intelligence, and profitability. Hypothesis testing is one of the key processes in quantitative investigations because it enables examining the above-mentioned theoretical frameworks and identifying whether or not the data support the proposed dependent and independent variables \autocite{Creswell2018}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Testing hypothesised relationships}
\end{itemize}
When testing relationships between concepts, one must verify the relationships indicated in the conceptual scheme against empirical data. In quantitative research, hypotheses are usually derived from the structure theories provided by literature. Thus, in quantitative studies, the researcher can state whether any relationships exist between such variables as organisational culture, leadership style, AI adoption, or ROI, etc. Hypothesis testing in quantitative research is of utmost importance since it allows testing theoretical schemes \autocite{Creswell2018}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Measuring latent variables}
\end{itemize}
Latent constructs are described as hypothetical concepts that cannot be measured using direct observations. However, multidimensional indicators show its significance. This research is utilising the termologу utilised in the technical literature and applies it to organisational culture, leadership style, and effectiveness of AI implementation, as all of them represent complex behaviour of an organisation. This implies that investigation of the mentioned constructs was possible due to utilising a structured questionnaire and special scales developed for this purpose \autocite{Hair2019}.







\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Estimating causal processes}
\end{itemize}
Estimating causal pathways requires consideration of how one variable influences another within a theoretical framework. In this study, the causal pathways of interest describe the influence of organisational culture and leadership on the effective adoption of AI coding assistants, and the subsequent influence of adoption on productivity and ROI outcomes. Structural equation modelling (SEM) was used to examine these relationships simultaneously, allowing both the direct and indirect effects among variables to be estimated and providing a clearer understanding of the mechanisms through which these relationships operate \autocite{Hair2019}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Generating generalisable findings}
\end{itemize}
The primary goal of quantitative research is to yield results which are applicable to a larger population than the sample considered. In this study, a large enough sample of software engineering professionals had been drawn in order to make the findings generalisable to a larger group of organisations. Statistical tools had also been applied to calculate the probability of the observed associations being real effect as compared to the random chance event, thus bringing important evidence both for practitioners and researchers \autocite{Bryman2016}.

The use of structured surveys and objective performance measures is consistent with the post-positivist emphasis on measurement \autocite{Hair2019}.

\section{Research Design}
\label{sec:Research Design}
\subsection{Quantitative Explanatory Design}
\label{sec:Quantitative Explanatory Design}
The research utilised a cross-sectional quantitative approach to investigate the proposed links between organisational culture, leadership practices, efficient AI adoption, productivity, and return on investment.

Quantitative techniques are ideally suited to the acquisition of knowledge about the nature of the relationships between variables and the verification of theoretical models \autocite{Bryman2016}. Since the conceptual framework indicated the existence of mediating relationships, the study was able to use the strengths of structural equation modelling (SEM) which allows for simultaneous examination of the relationships under consideration , evaluation of the direct, indirect, and total effects of the variables \autocite{Hair2019}.

\section{Research Hypotheses}
\label{sec:Research Hypotheses}
Consistent with the conceptual framework and the post-positivist emphasis on testing hypothesised relationships, the following hypotheses were formulated to guide the structural equation modelling analysis:

\begin{itemize}
	\item[\textbf{H1:}] Organisational culture has a significant positive influence on the effective adoption of AI coding assistants.
	\item[\textbf{H2:}] Leadership practices have a significant positive influence on the effective adoption of AI coding assistants.
	\item[\textbf{H3:}] The effective adoption of AI coding assistants has a significant positive influence on software engineering productivity.
	\item[\textbf{H4:}] Improvements in software engineering productivity have a significant positive influence on the return on investment realised from AI coding assistants.
	\item[\textbf{H5:}] The effective adoption of AI coding assistants mediates the relationship between (a) organisational culture and (b) leadership practices, and the productivity and ROI outcomes associated with AI coding assistants.
\end{itemize}

The hypotheses reflect the key proposition of the research study, namely, that organisational and leadership variables do not have a direct impact on ROI, but instead act through determining the degree to which coding assistants powered by AI technology have been suitably utilised in programming teams. \section{Population and Sampling}
\label{sec:Population and Sampling}
\subsection{Target Population}
\label{sec:Target Population}
The study focused on software engineering teams within large organisations in South Africa that used AI coding assistants.

\subsection{Sampling Strategy}
\label{sec:Sampling Strategy}
In this study, purposive sampling was used to select organisations that met the most important of the following criteria:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Organisations that use AI coding assistants;
	\item Organisations that have used AI coding assistants for no less than six months; and
	\item Organisations that have identifiable software engineering metrics.	
\end{itemize}

The sample comprised software developers and team leads of the selected organisations and was developed by applying a stratified sampling approach.

Guidelines suggest that in order to allow structural equation modelling to be applied successfully, having 200 and more respondents in the sample is critical \autocite{Hair2019}. Hence, the goal of this study was to attain a sample size between 250 and 400 respondents.

\section{Measurement and Operationalisation}
\label{sec:Measurement and Operationalisation}
Measurement scales were developed for each latent construct to establish construct validity \autocite{DeVellis2022}.

\subsection{Organisational Culture}
\label{sec:Organisational Culture}
\textbf{Organisational culture} was assessed using adapted and validated scales addressing the following dimensions:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Psychological Safety}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Psychological safety} refers to the degree of comfort felt by members of a group to experiment, question, and contribute ideas without fear of criticism. In software engineering teams using AI coding assistants such as GitHub Copilot, psychological safety enables developers to question the suggestions generated by the tool. Developers who feel safe experimenting with the tool are better placed to become proficient in its use and to draw effectively on generative AI. Literature on the use of generative AI in knowledge work indicates that productivity gains are more pronounced where workers engage with such tools collaboratively \autocite{Noy2023}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Learning Orientation}
\end{itemize}
The term \textbf{learning orientation} refers to the priority given by an organisation to continuous learning and the development of skills. When generative AI technologies are adopted in organisations, developers have to equip themselves with capabilities that will enable them to utilise them effectively.Those organisations that have a solid learning orientation will have an advantage over others in implementing generative AI.Studies conducted in real settings have shown that productivity of workers can be increased in a considerable way with the help of generative AI technologies \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Knowledge Sharing}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Knowledge sharing} refers to the transfer of information and skills between members of the team. This usually involves the transferring of knowledge about the prompts that are required for triggering the needed outputs, methods for debugging, and other best practices of the tool usage. This process helps achieve more efficient application of generative AI by the team. Empirical evidence from real-life use of AI coding assistants shows that the development teams benefit from using knowledge sharing in their application of such tools \autocite{Sergeyuk2025}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Digital Openness}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Digital openness} describes the readiness of organisations and their personnel to embrace new digital technologies. Organisations that demonstrate digital openness will be more receptive to the use of generative AI tools and will modify their working processes to suit the  technological innovations.  In software engineering, digital openness may promote the use of AI-based coding assistants as well as assist developers in searching alternative means of speeding up their workflow.

Likert scales were used to measure perceptions and attitudes \autocite{Bryman2016}.

\subsection{Leadership Practices}
\label{sec:Leadership Practices}
Leadership practices were assessed using scales covering the following dimensions:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Executive Sponsorship}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Executive sponsorship} refers to the level of support that an organisations senior leadership provides for the implementation of generative AI tools within the organisation. When leaders publicise their support for AI coding assistants, they let employees' know that AI is of great importance to the organisation. Research on generative AI adoption suggests that employees are more likely to work with generative AI technologies if they receive support from their organisations leadership \autocite{Noy2023}. Research on AI adoption indicates that executive sponsorship allows for successful coordination of the implementation of AI technologies \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Governance Clarity}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Governance clarity} means having clear policies and rules regarding how AI technologies are to be used. Using technologies such as Copilot has large implications for the company's governance framework with respect to IP issues, security, and software quality assurance, making it vital for companies to have robust governance structures in place in order to guide the use of AI code and make responsibilities clear. The studies conducted in the area of AI governance show that companies with a well-defined governance framework have greater chances of successfully overcoming technology risks while obtaining the necessary productivity \autocite{Hradecky2022}, which makes governance clarity an important factor in the sustainable utilisation of generative AI tools in the sphere of software development.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Resource Support}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Resource support} refers to the financial, technical, and human resources committed to the successful deployment of AI technologies. Deploying AI coding assistants requires investment in developer training, integration into development environments, infrastructure, and ongoing monitoring; where this support is absent, organisations risk failing to realise the productivity benefits the tools can offer. Empirical research on organisational investment in AI suggests that organisations investing in developer training and digital infrastructure are more likely to achieve performance benefits from their AI investment. \textcite{Babina2024} argue that sufficient resource support is crucial to the productive deployment of AI coding assistants.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Metric Alignment}
\end{itemize}
Metric alignment refers to the alignment of organisational performance metrics with the intended outcomes of AI adoption. For AI coding assistants, these outcomes typically centre on improved productivity, shorter cycle times, and better software quality, and it is therefore important that performance metrics, such as deployment frequency, defect rates, and development throughput, are aligned with them. Where performance metrics are aligned with the intended outcomes of AI adoption, employees' are more likely to direct their use of AI towards those organisational outcomes. Research on generative AI productivity indicates that organisational outcomes become tangible once performance indicators are aligned with AI usage and task efficiency \autocite{Noy2023}.

\subsection{Effective Adoption}
\label{sec:Effective Adoption}
The term effective adoption refers to the integration of AI coding assistants in software development and their usage by software programmers. Adoption does not imply simply executing a tool; rather, it indicates how the programmer operatively utilises the tool, trusts it, and incorporates it into everyday work activities. The empirical studies conducted on the AI coding assistants indicate that AI coding assistants are able to enhance developer performance to some extent depending on how efficiently they apply this tool in their work activities \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}. This framework was analysed based on the following parameters:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Usage Intensity}
\end{itemize}
Usage frequency refers to how often a developer uses the coding assistant. Frequent interaction with the tool generally results in the developer’s proficiency in the tool and finding ways to become productive. A high degree of usage frequency indicates that a developer has adapted the tool into their everyday workflow. Studies on generative AI in productivity in knowledge work have shown that high frequencies of usage lead to productivity and efficiency increases \autocite{Noy2023}.
can
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Workflow Integration}
\end{itemize}
The term \textbf{"workflow integration"} describes how well the AI coding assistants fit within the software development process, including the environment, code review and teamwork. The tools which are better fitted into the existing workflow will be more accepted by the developers. Some studies conducted in the area of AI-enabled work showed that the best results can be received when the new developments are integrated into the existing processes rather than those, which disrupt them \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Trust Calibration}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Trust calibration} is the ability of the developers' to build the correct level of trust in outcomes coming from the AI system. The developers' need to decide when a suggestion made by the AI system should be trusted. If one trusts the recommendation too much, this will lead to mistakes; however, high scepticism is not good as well because it prevents improvement of project efficiency \autocite{Noy2023}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Feature Utilisation Depth}
\end{itemize}
The depth of usage of the feature pertains to the degree of the usage of the sophisticated functionalities of AI-assisted coding tools by the programming professionals instead of the utilisation of only basic coding features. In general, generative AI tools come with a wide range of features which include coding, debugging, documentation, and testing, and as such, the use of greater number of functionalities is linked to hitting greater productivity. Studies on AI supported software development have indicated that the higher the level of engagement with AI functionalities, the better the development and problem solving processes \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}.


\section{Data Collection}
\label{sec:Data Collection}
Data was collected through a structured online questionnaire. Online questionnaires have become popular in organisation and information systems research because of their ability to reach large samples. The questionnaire contained closed-ended questions arranged according to Likert-type scales to measure concepts like organisational culture, leadership behaviour, successful adoption of AI programming assistants and company results.

The questionnaires were sent out through professional networks and communities related to software engineering and technology. Participation was voluntary and was done based on the informed consent obtained from the respondents before the research started.

The answers included in the questionnaire were modified versions of methods that were used and approved in earlier studies. The following constructs were analysed in the study subjective support of the leaders, organisational culture, AI adoption behaviour and the result type variable.

Various studies dealing with AI adoption have shown that success of AI tools does not depend only on technology, but also on the correct way of adoption and usage by people and organisations \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}. This is the reason why the research assessed both the active and passive usage of AI.

\subsection{Pilot Testing}
\label{sec:Pilot Testing}
Prior to launching the main survey, a preliminary test was conducted with a small number of software engineering practitioners who didn’t take part in the main survey. The preliminary test evaluated whether the survey was clear, whether it was too lengthy, and whether the survey questions could be understood by the survey participants. After making the necessary improvements, the survey was conducted with the actual participants \autocite{DeVellis2022}.

\section{Data Analysis}
\label{sec:Data Analysis}
The collected data was analysed using SmartPLS and SPSS statistical software's capable of performing sophisticated statistical procedures such as factor analysis and structural equation modelling, a characteristic that would allow the researchers to conduct complex statistical operations such as implementing factor analysis and structural equation modelling. The analysis was performed in accordance with the following stages.

Stage one consisted of descriptive statistics that enabled the researchers to characterise the sample and compute the means, standard deviations and frequency distributions. The completion of the first stage allowed the researcher to identify missing values and outliers prior to conducting more advanced statistical evaluation.

The second stage consisted of reliability and validity testing. Cronbach's alpha was computed to measure the internal quality of the measurement used to measure organisational culture, leadership and successful implementation of AI coding assistants; the value over the threshold of 0.70 is known to be good enough \autocite{Hair 2019}. Thereafter, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was employed to establish the validity of the measurement and verify that the survey questions reflected the theoretical constructs included in the conceptual framework, a critical step in the study involving new technologies such as generative AI considering broad organisational and behavioral issues connected to innovation flows \autocite{Mikalef 2021}.

The third stage consisted of examining the relationships as described in the conceptual framework through the use of structural equation modeling (SEM). SEM was applicable for this research as it allows testing multiple relationships between the variables under examination and provides estimates for both direct and indirect effects, which is necessary when studying the complex relationships between organisational factors and technological outcomes that have been more typical in a field of information systems and technology adoption \autocite{Hair2019}. In particular, SEM helped in studying the influence of organisational culture and leadership practices on the successful implementation of AI coding assistants in software engineering teams and its implications for the productivity level and ROI of AI investment. This was based on the literature that suggested that generative AI has a great potential for improving productivity levels in knowledge-based work \autocite{Noy2023} and stressed the importance of proper incorporation of these technologies into the working processes \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}.

To test the mediating effects of organisational culture and leadership practices proposed in the conceptual framework (Section~\ref{sec:Research Hypotheses}, H5), bootstrapping was used. Bootstrapping is a resampling procedure in which the dataset is repeatedly resampled to test the statistical significance of indirect relationships between variables, and is widely used in mediation analysis because it produces more reliable confidence intervals for indirect effects \autocite{Hair2019}.

Collectively, this analytical strategy was designed to generate empirical evidence on how organisational culture and leadership practices shaped the adoption of AI coding assistants, and how this adoption influenced the return on investment realised from AI technology in software engineering teams.

\section{Research Limitations}
\label{sec:Research Limitations}
As is the case in any research, methodological limitations need to be addressed. To begin with, the study uses a cross-sectional design where the relationships between variables are studied at one particular point in time. This means that while SEM allows for testing the hypothesised causal relations, it may not produce results as reliable as longitudinal or experimental designs \autocite{Creswell2018}. Second, the use of self-report measures in assessing culture, leadership, adoption, and productivity runs the risk of producing common method bias due to the potential for over or under-reporting by the participants \autocite{Bryman2016}. Third, the resulted sampling method, although suitable for recruiting organisations under the criteria of the research, limits the generalisation of results to large South African organisations that have at least six months of experience with AI coding assistants. Finally, the ROI was operationalised using perception and productivity variables rather than financial data which makes it difficult to assess the actual financial return the participants got. These shortcomings will be discussed again in Chapter Five along with the suggestions for further research.

\section{Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:Ethical Considerations}
This study included ethical considerations. The survey was voluntary and the aim of the research was explained to the respondents beforehand. The respondents were assured of anonymity and confidentiality of their responses.

No personal identifiers were required from the respondents. The information received was used solely for academic reasons, and it was stored following the guidelines of social research.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:Summary}
In this chapter, the approach applied in this study was outlined. The investigation was based on the quantitative design to discover the relationships between organisational culture, leadership behaviours, efficient usage of AI coding assistants, and return on investment. The data has been collected via surveying software engineering experts using a structured questionnaire, and statistical methods, such as structural equations modelling, were applied to approve the existence of the hypothesised relations in the conceptual framework.

With the help of this methodology this study has attempted to gain practical knowledge regarding the impact of organisational and leadership aspects on the benefits that are achieved through applying generative AI technologies in software engineering.
