\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\gls{genai} coding assistants have been shown to accelerate individual programming tasks, but faster task completion does not necessarily produce a \gls{roi} for the organisations that adopt these tools. This study examined how \gls{it} leadership and organisational culture are associated with the perceived \gls{roi} of \gls{ai} coding assistants among South African software engineering practitioners, and whether these associations are mediated by the assimilation quality of the tools, that is, the extent to which they are integrated productively into software engineering work. The study was 
grounded in \gls{it} value realisation theory and socio-technical systems theory.

A research design that is quantitative, cross-sectional and confirmatory was chosen, based on a post-positivist paradigm. The data was gathered via an online questionnaire, which was sent out using professional contacts, \gls{it} professionals' associations and technology forums online, with a final sample of 354 respondents whose teams utilized AI coding assistants. In this study, six hypotheses were analyzed using \gls{pls-sem}, with organisational readiness, team size, 
developer experience and adoption duration as control variables.

Organisational culture was positively associated with assimilation quality ($\beta = .579$, $p < .001$), and assimilation quality was positively associated with perceived \gls{roi} ($\beta = .356, $p = .015). The association between organisational culture and perceived \gls{roi} operated entirely through assimilation quality (indirect effect = .206, 95\% \gls{ci} [.025, .409]), indicating indirect-only mediation. \gls{it} Leadership was not found to be significantly related to either assimilation quality or \gls{roi} when organisational culture was included in the model, an outcome that was due to considerable empirically-based overlap between the two concepts. Organisational readiness, included as a control variable, was the strongest predictor of perceived \gls{roi} ($\beta = .588$, $p < .001$). The model explained 81.6\% of the variance in perceived \gls{roi}. Because all constructs were measured by self-report and most responses were concentrated at the most positive end of the scale, the magnitudes of these estimates should be interpreted with caution.

The study contributes to \gls{it} business value research by showing that, in this sample, organisational culture was associated with perceived \gls{roi} through the quality with which \gls{ai} coding assistants were assimilated into engineering practice. For \gls{it} leaders, the findings suggest that investment in organisational readiness and in the assimilation of \gls{ai} coding assistants into everyday workflows should be prioritised, supported by a culture of learning, experimentation and responsible use.
