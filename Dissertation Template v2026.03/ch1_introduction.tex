\section{Introduction} 
\label{sec:Introduction} 
This chapter introduces a study that examined \emph{the role of \gls{it} leadership in realising the \gls{roi} of \gls{ai} coding assistants}, as perceived by software engineering practitioners in \gls{sa}. The chapter begins with setting the background for the research and the research problem that the study sought to address. Thereafter, the research objective, aim, and the research questions are set out, and how each research question was addressed through the hypotheses or analysis is shown. The chapter thereafter discusses the importance, scope, limitations, and delimitations of the study, as well as the theoretical framework on which the research was based. The chapter ends with a brief discussion of the structure of the dissertation.

\section{Background} 
\label{sec:Background} 

\gls{ai} has been described as a general-purpose technology whose productivity benefits depend on substantial complementary investment in organisational processes, skills and management practices \parencite{Brynjolfsson2021, Babina2024}. Since the public release of large language model tools in late 2022, \gls{genai} has moved from experimentation towards routine use in knowledge work \parencite{Gillespie2025, Hughes2026}. Organisations have invested in these tools in the expectation of efficiency gains and improved process performance, yet surveys of managers indicate that only a minority of organisations report significant financial benefits from their \gls{ai} initiatives \parencite{Ransbotham2020}. \gls{is} research has accordingly shifted its emphasis from whether organisations adopt \gls{ai} to how, and under which organisational conditions, \gls{ai} investments create business value \parencite{Enholm2022, Berente2021, Mikalef2021}.

In software engineering, \gls{ai} coding assistants are among the most visible applications of \gls{genai}. These tools are embedded in the development environment and provide context-sensitive code completion, generate whole functions, explain and document code, and support debugging \parencite{LiangJT2024, Sergeyuk2025}. Experimental and field studies report that these tools can accelerate programming work. In a controlled experiment, developers with access to GitHub Copilot completed a defined programming task 55.8\% faster than a control group \parencite{Peng2023}. Field experiments at three large companies found that access to an \gls{ai} coding assistant increased the number of completed tasks by approximately 26\% on average \parencite{Cui2026}, and analyses of telemetry from a large user population linked the acceptance of \gls{ai} suggestions to developers' perceived productivity \parencite{Ziegler2024}. Comparable gains have been reported for other forms of knowledge work \parencite{Noy2023, Brynjolfsson2025}. Adoption has since become widespread: the 2025 DORA research programme reported that 90\% of the software development professionals it surveyed used \gls{ai} \parencite{DORA2025}, and 84\% of respondents to the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey used or planned to use \gls{ai} tools in their development process \parencite{StackOverflow2025}.

Productivity gains demonstrated in controlled settings, however, do not necessarily translate into sustained organisational performance. Economic research shows that the gains from \gls{ai} often fail to appear in organisational performance measures until complementary changes in processes, skills and capabilities have been made \parencite{Brynjolfsson2021}. In a 2025 global survey, 88\% of respondents reported regular use of \gls{ai} in at least one business function, but only 39\% reported any impact of \gls{ai} on earnings at the enterprise level \parencite{McKinsey2025}. Firm-level studies likewise indicate that the returns from \gls{ai} investment are heterogeneous and depend on complementary organisational capabilities \parencite{Babina2024, Mikalef2021}. Industry evidence from software delivery points in the same direction: the 2024 DORA research programme found that higher \gls{ai} adoption was associated with improvements in documentation quality and code review speed, but also with small estimated decreases in software delivery throughput and stability \parencite{Harvey2024}.

The evidence on productivity gains is also far from uniform. In the field experiments reported by \textcite{Cui2026}, less experienced developers benefited considerably more than senior developers. A randomised controlled trial with experienced open-source developers working on mature code bases found that access to early-2025 \gls{ai} tools increased task completion time by 19\%, even though the developers themselves expected to be faster \parencite{Becker2025}. \textcite{DellAcqua2023} study similarly show that \gls{ai} improves performance on some tasks while degrading it on others. Developer trust also varies: in the 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey, more respondents distrusted the accuracy of \gls{ai} tools (46\%) than trusted it (33\%) \parencite{StackOverflow2025}. Quality and risk concerns add further variation: \gls{ai}-generated code has been shown to contain security weaknesses \parencite{Pearce2022}, developers using \gls{ai} assistants have been found to write less secure code while believing it to be more secure \parencite{Perry2023}, and the correctness of generated code varies considerably across tasks \parencite{MoradiDakhel2023}. Taken together, these studies indicate that the value of \gls{ai} coding assistants depends on how they are used and managed, not only on the capability of the technology.

Organisational culture and leadership are recognised as determinants of whether new technologies are successfully adopted and used \parencite{Johnk2021, Uren2023}. Organisational culture refers to the shared values, norms and practices that shape how members of an organisation interpret and respond to new technology \parencite{Murire2024, An2024}. In digitally oriented organisations, a culture that supports experimentation, learning and evidence-based decision making is associated with a more positive orientation towards technology adoption \parencite{An2024, FossoWamba2024}. Recent empirical studies report that digital organisational culture strengthens the link between human \gls{ai} skills and innovation outcomes \parencite{An2024}, and that leadership, governance and employee engagement shape how \gls{ai} is embedded in organisational work \parencite{Hossain2025, Murire2024, Schneider2023}. Despite this growing recognition, research on \gls{ai} adoption remains fragmented \parencite{Romeo2025, Enholm2022}. Studies have tended either to examine \gls{ai} adoption and value at the level of the firm \parencite{Babina2024, Mikalef2021, Berente2021} or to measure effects on individual task performance \parencite{Peng2023, Noy2023, Brynjolfsson2025, Cui2026}. Comparatively few studies have integrated organisational culture, leadership and ROI in the specific context of software engineering.

African organisations have also begun adopting \gls{genai}, partly to address resource constraints and operational inefficiencies. A global survey reported that respondents in emerging economies use \gls{ai} more frequently and hold more positive views of its potential to improve productivity than respondents in advanced economies \parencite{Gillespie2025}. At the same time, the literature points to difficulties with digital skills pipelines, governance maturity and leadership capability in the adoption of \gls{genai} \parencite{Hughes2026, Isagah2024}. Without strategy, trust, and a learning orientation, the adoption of \gls{ai} is likely to be purely symbolic \parencite{Ransbotham2020}. The African setting, therefore, becomes an appropriate setting for analysing how socio-organisational factors affect the benefits that organisations derive from \gls{ai} technology.

In \gls{sa}, research has been conducted on the impact of \gls{ai} on organisations and labour. There has been research in South African organisations on the effects of the implementation of \gls{ai} on work and organisational culture  \parencite{Murire2024}, the perception of human resource managers on the effects of \gls{ai} on employees \parencite{Poisat2024}, and the effects of organizational factors like leadership and readiness on adopting new technology in the finance industry \parencite{Matsepe2022}. Further studies have proposed frameworks for \gls{ai} adoption that emphasise the interplay between people and technology \parencite{Smit2024}, and qualitative evidence from South African small and medium-sized enterprises suggests that \gls{genai} improves operational efficiency but is constrained by skills shortages, set-up costs and data-security concerns \parencite{Muzuva2024}. International reviews reach similar conclusions: the organisational benefits of \gls{ai} depend on alignment between the technology and the organisation’s culture, structure and leadership \parencite{Romeo2025, Kassa2025}. In software engineering specifically, \gls{genai} tools have been reported to improve coding productivity when they are integrated into engineering practice \parencite{Alenezi2025, Sergeyuk2025}. For software engineering teams that use \gls{ai} coding assistants, leadership and culture can therefore be expected to influence whether productivity gains are realised as productivity, quality and financial returns.

\section{Problem statement} 
\label{sec:Problem statement} 

\gls{genai} was initially used mainly for information retrieval and content generation, but its use has extended into the core processes of knowledge work. Experimental evidence shows that \gls{genai} improves the speed and quality of professional writing tasks \parencite{Noy2023}, and field evidence shows productivity improvements among customer-support agents \parencite{Brynjolfsson2025}. Studies in software development report similar effects on task completion: field experiments with GitHub Copilot found increases in completed tasks \parencite{Cui2026}, and a controlled experiment found that developers completed a programming task substantially faster with an \gls{ai} pair programmer \parencite{Peng2023}. These studies, however, measured task speed and output, not \gls{roi}. Faster completion of a task is a necessary but not a sufficient condition for a return on the organisation's investment, because the costs of licences, training, integration, review and governance, and the effects on quality and rework, determine whether time savings become organisational value \parencite{Enholm2022, Mikalef2021}.

The literature also shows that the performance effects of \gls{ai} coding assistants vary with the context in which they are used. Code generated by GitHub Copilot has been found to vary in correctness and maintainability and to require review effort \parencite{MoradiDakhel2023}, \gls{ai}  assistance has been associated with security weaknesses \parencite{Pearce2022, Perry2023}, and experienced developers working on familiar code bases have been slowed down rather than accelerated \parencite{Becker2025}. Research on \gls{ai}  governance argues that sustained value creation requires accountability structures and leadership oversight that turn experimentation into lasting performance improvement \parencite{Mantymaki2022, Schneider2023}. Studies of organisational innovation with \gls{genai} further show that performance effects differ according to whether organisations use \gls{ai}  for automation or augmentation, a choice shaped by leadership and culture \parencite{Holmstrom2025}. Research on organisational \gls{ai}  adoption adds that many organisations acquire or pilot \gls{ai} without integrating it deeply into work practices \parencite{Uren2023, Ransbotham2020}, and that top management support and organisational readiness are central to moving from experimentation to routine use \parencite{Johnk2021, Weber2023}.

These processes are particularly relevant in Africa, where most organisations face some challenges with technological adaptation, skills development and governance. \gls{ai} readiness research in Africa highlights the importance of organisational capabilty in the transition from \gls{ai} pilots to performace outcomes \parencite{Isagah2024}. In \gls{sa}, the doption of merging technologies have been shown to be influenced mostly by leadership support and organisational readiness \parencite{Matsepe2022}, and sociotechnical considerations and change management have been identified as central to \gls{ai} adoption \parencite{Smit2024}. Empirical evidence on whether, and through which mechanisms, South African software engineering teams realise returns from \gls{ai} coding assistants nevertheless remains scarce \parencite{Poisat2024, Muzuva2024}.

The problem addressed by this study was therefore as follows: although \gls{ai} coding assistants have been shown to accelerate individual programming tasks, organisations that invest in them have little empirical evidence on whether, and through which organisational mechanisms, these task-level gains translate into a return on investment. In particular, the extent to which \gls{it} leadership and organisational culture influence the \gls{roi} of \gls{ai} coding assistants, and whether they do so by improving how deeply the tools are assimilated into software engineering practice, had not been empirically examined in South African software engineering teams. Without such evidence, \gls{it} leaders cannot determine which organisational conditions to prioritise when seeking value from their investment in \gls{ai} coding assistants.

\section{Significance/rationale} 
\label{sec:Significance/rationale} 
\gls{genai} has a potential to enhance productivity in software engineering and efficiency in knowledge work \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025, Peng2023}. \gls{ai} coding assistants speed up the software development process and make the completion of the task more probable. Nevertheless, even though the productivity has increased, the connection between the use of \gls{ai} coding assistants and the \gls{roi} obtained by software engineering teams remains unclear.

Leadership and organisational culture have been identified as factors that affect the successful adoption of the associated benefits of using \gls{ai} technology. It affects innovation through guiding organisational resources towards innovative practices \autocite{Hossain2025}. A culture of learning, cooperation, and innovation is essential for organisational digital capability \autocite{AbdulWahab2024, Li2025, Poisat2024}. A system perspective, adaptability, is essential for organisational assimilation of digital technologies. Performance of emerging markets is dependent on their level of readiness and leadership context\autocite{Gillespie2025, Nurlia2023}.

However, a link still seems to be missing since there is no literature link between organisational \gls{roi} and the productivity of \gls{ai} coding assistants. Likewise, there is no literature link between organisational \gls{roi} and \gls{ai} value. Finally, the literature on organisational culture and leadership does not have any literature link between organisational \gls{roi} and \gls{ai} coding assistants' value for software engineering teams. This study fills in this gap since it will explore the influence of organisational culture and leadership on \gls{roi} of \gls{ai} coding assistants for software engineering teams.

\subsection{Scope} 
\label{sec:Scope} 
This study is guided by a quantitative research approach that seeks to explore the impact of organisational culture and leadership on the achievement of \gls{roi} for \gls{ai} coding assistants in software engineering teams. The study is limited to the usage of \gls{genai} tools that facilitate the inclusion of code, inline suggestions, debugging, and documentation, as they are part of the software development process. Literature has already proven that the usage of such tools increases productivity as well as the efficiency of task completion \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025, Noy2023, Peng2023}.

The target population is software engineering teams working for medium-to-large-scale organisations in \gls{sa} who are using \gls{ai} coding assistants tools. The reasonable time frame for evaluating the outcomes varies for each company since the adoption of \gls{ai} coding assistants is dependent on firm-level characteristics \autocite{Babina2024}. Moreover, the digital readiness of the African continent is recognised as an environmental factor \autocite{Gillespie2025}.

The scope of independent variables is limited to organisational culture and leadership in this study, as they might impact the achievement of \gls{roi} for \gls{ai} coding assistants. The organisational culture is analysed by taking the learning orientation, collaboration, and innovation support as factors \autocite{AbdulWahab2024, FossoWamba2024}. Leadership is analysed by taking the strategic direction, governance, and digital support as factors \autocite{An2024, Hossain2025, Murire2024}. The \gls{roi} is analysed by taking the quantified performance indicators as factors.

%  Delimitation

\subsection{Delimitation} 
\label{sec:Delimitation} 
Because the technical efficiency of \gls{ai} tools has already been studied in experiments  \autocite{Peng2023}, it is important to highlight that this research does not focus on the accuracy benchmarking, technical algorithmic evaluation of the efficiency of the \gls{ai} coding assistants, architecture of the \gls{ai} model, or other technical evaluations of the efficiency. While in this research, there was a choice to focus on the socio-organisational factors of value realisation in the context of \gls{ai}. The research only includes software engineering teams, not other professional groups using \gls{genai}, such as marketing, finance, or legal teams. This is because the software engineering workflow is significantly different from that of other professions.

The significance of leadership and organisational culture is considered in the study as the main independent elements of the research. However, some variables which also could have an impact on \gls{roi}, including national regulations, macro environment, and pricing models have not been analysed properly. While it is evident that those variables can considerably influence \gls{roi}, the objective of the current research is to focus on only internal variables.

\subsection{Brief Limitations}
\label{sec:Brief Limitations}
There are several constraints that are inherent in this study. To begin with, the calculation of \gls{roi} will rely somewhat on self-reported perceptions of productivity and quality. Secondly, the study is limited in terms of violations of generalisability by virtue of its focus on South African organisations. Lastly, the cross-sectional or short-term longitudinal aspect of the design will be without power in terms of the long-term impact on financial results because the effect of the culture and leadership on the productivity might take a long time to manifest itself \autocite{An2024}.

\section{Theoretical and conceptual framework} 
\label{sec:Theoretical and conceptual framework} 
This study is based on the following premise: while the productivity gains derived from the use of \gls{ai} coding assistants can be significant for developers, the value that can be derived from the productivity gains for the organisation itself remains an open question. This view is consistent with the value realisation framework \autocite{Kuna2014}, which argues that business value depends on how the organisation manages, supports, and integrates the technology into its day-to-day business processes \autocite{Mikalef2021}. It can also connect with the sociotechnical systems perspective noting that, value derived from the utilisation of \gls{ai} coding assistants depends on the people, processes, leaders, and organisations involved.

There is a significant body of research that suggests that \gls{genai} has a positive impact on individual performance. Research conducted in the field has shown that \gls{genai} has a positive effect on the productivity and quality of performance \autocite{Brynjolfsson2025}. Experimental research has also shown that \gls{genai} has a positive effect on the efficiency and quality of performance \autocite{Noy2023}. Research has shown that programmers working with \gls{ai} assistants perform their tasks more efficiently and with better quality than those programmers who do not use \gls{ai} assistants \autocite{Peng2023}. However, research conducted at the firm level has shown that the \gls{roi} generated by \gls{ai} is firm-specific, depending upon how the firm invests in \gls{ai} \autocite{Babina2024}.

In this context, leadership and organisational culture are identified as key factors that explain the disparities identified. Leadership is responsible for guiding and directing the firm, as well as the way \gls{ai} coding assistants are used within the firm. The empirical evidence suggests that leadership is a key factor that affects digital or \gls{ai} performance outcomes  \autocite{Hossain2025, Murire2024}. On the other hand, organisational culture is responsible for influencing the behavioural aspects of individuals within the firm. Learning culture is a culture that promotes the use of \gls{ai} tools within the firm through collaboration and innovation. The empirical evidence suggests that a digital organisational culture strengthens the relationship between \gls{ai} performance outcomes and organisational performance \autocite{FossoWamba2024}. Furthermore, a digital organisational culture increases innovation outcomes by human-\gls{ai} capabilities \autocite{An2024}.


The conceptual model in question defines \gls{roi} from \gls{ai} coding assistants as the "key variable" of particular interest are the roles of \gls{it} leadership and organisational culture in relation to \gls{roi} as the explaining variables in the model. In addition, the importance of assimilation quality is explained in the context of the mediating role of leadership and organisational culture in their effect on \gls{roi}. Assimilation quality is understood as the stage in which \gls{ai} coding assistants are integrated into the process of software engineering and start being used in existing workflows. Furthermore, organisational readiness is included in the model as a control variable, the potential for successful implementation of \gls{ai} coding assistants would depend on the level of organisational readiness to adopt and work with digital technologies in general. Even though there is available literature reporting on higher performance on the level of individuals and tasks, little is known about the relationship between the achieved productivity level and the \gls{roi} from \gls{ai} coding assistants on the level of organisations \autocite{Nurlia2023, Gillespie2025}.

\textbf{Chapter 1: Introduction} \newline
Chapter 1 presents the basis of the research that investigates The role of \gls{it} leadership in realising the \gls{roi} of \gls{ai} coding assistants. It provides the research problem, objectives, questions and significance of the study, as well as its scope and limitations. The section also reflects on the theoretical framework of this dissertation as well as the outline of the entire work.

\textbf{Chapter 2: Literature review} \newline
Chapter 2 outlines the existing knowledge in the field of \gls{genai}, \gls{ai} coding tools, productivity, organisations returns on investments, leaders, organisational culture, governance, and \gls{ai} assimilation. It looks into the relationship between productivity at task level and value at the organisational level and identifies the research gaps in the existing literature. Finally, the chapter introduces the theoretical perspectives that will be used in the study.

\textbf{Chapter 3: Methodology} \newline
Chapter 3 discusses the research methodology used in the study. It describes the research philosophy and approach, design, the sample population, and data gathering technique, and data analysis through \gls{pls-sem}.

\textbf{Chapter 4: Results} \newline
Chapter 4 presents the findings of the research study. It presents the study’s details, researcher characteristics, descriptive statistics, reliability and validity test of instruments, structural modeling and analysis of the relationship between the variables \gls{it} leadership, culture, \gls{ai} coding assistant use, and \gls{roi}.

\textbf{Chapter 5: Discussion} \newline
Chapter 5 the findings are interpreted in the light of the research questions, research objectives, theoretical framework and existing literature. It talks about the relations discovered in the analysis and explains what information the findings disclose about the conditions necessary for obtaining \gls{roi} from \gls{ai} coding assistants in software engineering teams. 

\textbf{Chapter 6: Conclusion} \newline
